The whistleblowers

The people whose testimony shaped the UAP debate: their strongest statements, what can be checked, and the pushback. Most of the recent testimony was given under oath to Congress. None of it has yet been backed by public physical evidence, and we don't treat it as proof either way.

David GruschBob LazarLuis ElizondoDavid FravorRyan GravesTim Gallaudet

David Grusch

Former Air Force intelligence officer; served on the Pentagon's UAP Task Force

Sworn testimony, House Oversight, July 26, 2023

“I was informed, in the course of my official duties, of a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering program to which I was denied access.”

What he says

  • The U.S. has recovered craft of non-human origin and has run a secret program to reverse-engineer them for decades.
  • Asked whether bodies were recovered, he said "non-human" remains came with some recoveries, according to people with direct knowledge he interviewed.
  • He says he interviewed about 40 witnesses over four years, and that retaliation followed his complaints.

What checks out

  • His military and intelligence career, including the UAP Task Force role, is documented.
  • The Intelligence Community Inspector General found his 2022 whistleblower complaint "credible and urgent," which means it warranted review, not that the claims were proven.

The pushback

  • The Pentagon said it found no "verifiable information" that such programs exist.
  • AARO's 2024 historical review found no evidence of recovered extraterrestrial craft or reverse-engineering programs.
  • Grusch has never claimed to have seen the craft himself; his account is based on what others told him, and he said details could only be shared in classified settings.

Status: Unverified. His claims fueled new disclosure legislation, but no physical evidence has been made public.

BL

Bob Lazar

Self-described physicist who says he worked at a secret site called "S-4" near Area 51

Interviews with KLAS-TV reporter George Knapp, 1989

What he says

  • In 1988–89 he worked at S-4, a hidden facility near Area 51, helping reverse-engineer the propulsion system of a flying disc "not made by human hands."
  • The craft were powered by a stable form of element 115 and used gravity manipulation.
  • He was intimidated after going public, and records of his education and work were erased.

What checks out

  • A 1982 Los Alamos Monitor article described him as working at the Los Alamos Meson Physics Facility, so he did have ties to the lab.
  • Element 115 was later created in a lab (2003) and named moscovium, which supporters count as a hit.

The pushback

  • No record has been found of the MIT and Caltech master's degrees he claims, and no professors or classmates have come forward.
  • Lab-made element 115 decays in a fraction of a second, unlike the stable fuel he described.
  • His S-4 work has never been corroborated by any other witness or document.

Status: Unverified and widely doubted, but still one of the most influential stories in ufology, and Lazar has never changed his account.

LE

Luis Elizondo

Former Pentagon intelligence official who says he ran the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)

Sworn testimony, House Oversight, November 13, 2024

“Let me be clear: UAP are real.”

What he says

  • "Advanced technologies not made by our government" are monitoring sensitive military installations around the world.
  • "The U.S. is in possession of UAP technologies, as are some of our adversaries."
  • If the technology belongs to an adversary, it would be "an intelligence failure eclipsing that of 9/11."

What checks out

  • He worked at the Pentagon and was involved in UAP work before resigning in 2017; his resignation helped trigger the 2017 New York Times story.
  • The Navy videos he pushed to release are real and were officially released in 2020.

The pushback

  • The Pentagon has given conflicting statements about his role in AATIP, and AARO says it found no evidence of hidden programs holding non-human technology.
  • Many of his strongest claims rely on classified information he says he can't share publicly.

Status: Disputed. A central figure in the disclosure movement; his claims about recovered technology remain unproven.

David Fravor

Retired Navy commander and F/A-18F pilot

Sworn testimony, House Oversight, July 26, 2023

“The technology that we faced was far superior to anything that we had.”

What he says

  • In November 2004, off San Diego, he and his wingmen saw a white, wingless, 40-foot "Tic Tac" object over a disturbance in the ocean.
  • When he descended toward it, it mirrored his moves, then accelerated away faster than anything he had seen.

What checks out

  • He is a firsthand witness, not relaying others' accounts, and other aircrew described the same encounter.
  • The FLIR1 video from the same event was officially released by the Pentagon.

The pushback

  • Skeptics argue the FLIR1 video alone could show a distant aircraft; the eyewitness accounts are what make the case unusual.
  • The object has never been identified, and no official explanation has been published.

Status: Officially unresolved. Widely considered the strongest military eyewitness case.

Ryan Graves

Former Navy F/A-18F pilot; founded Americans for Safe Aerospace

Sworn testimony, House Oversight, July 26, 2023

What he says

  • From 2014, Navy pilots off Virginia Beach saw unknown objects in their training airspace "every day for at least a couple years."
  • Some looked like dark gray or black cubes inside a clear sphere, and one passed within about 50 feet of two jets.
  • Pilots underreport sightings because of stigma, which he says is a flight-safety problem.

What checks out

  • He is a firsthand witness, and the Navy confirmed the pilots' reports at the time, issuing new reporting guidelines in 2019.
  • The GoFast and Gimbal videos come from the same period and region.

The pushback

  • The objects have not been identified; balloons have been suggested for some of the cube-in-sphere reports.
  • He reports what he and his squadron saw but makes no claims about their origin.

Status: Unresolved. His testimony focuses on safety and data, which made it one of the most credible at the hearing.

TG

Tim Gallaudet

Retired Navy rear admiral and former acting head of NOAA

Sworn testimony, House Oversight, November 13, 2024

“The very next day, the email disappeared from my account and those of the other recipients without explanation.”

What he says

  • In January 2015 he received an email with the GoFast video from a Fleet Forces Command operations officer during a Navy exercise.
  • He says the video showed "an unidentified object exhibiting flight and structural characteristics unlike anything in our arsenal."
  • The email then vanished from every recipient's inbox.

What checks out

  • His rank and roles are a matter of public record, and the GoFast video he describes was later released officially.

The pushback

  • The email itself has not been produced, and its deletion hasn't been confirmed by the Navy.
  • NASA's 2023 study team estimated the GoFast object was moving about 40 mph at around 13,000 feet, consistent with wind drift.

Status: Unverified. His account adds rank and detail, but the key evidence is missing.
