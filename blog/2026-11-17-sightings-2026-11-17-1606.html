Canadian Air Force Records Reveal 7 Unidentified Drone Incidents Over Two Bases

Two items made this scan: newly released Canadian military records on drones that crossed over air force bases, and fresh expert commentary on a "jellyfish" object filmed near an Alaska base.

RCAF records: seven unidentified drone incidents at CFB Cold Lake and CFB Trenton

What was reported: Royal Canadian Air Force reports obtained through an access to information request and reviewed by CTVNews.ca describe seven drone incidents in 2024 and 2025: four at CFB Cold Lake in Alberta and three at CFB Trenton in Ontario. The story was published on November 17, 2026.

  • On September 17, 2025, a camera-equipped drone flew over the Trenton runway while the base's counter-drone system was offline for a planned power outage. On September 22, the system reportedly failed to detect another drone.
  • At Cold Lake, one drone was estimated at 60 to 100 km/h, and a small white drone appeared twice within four days in September 2025. The reports say the base lacks a way to track small drones.
  • In all seven cases there was no attempt to intercept, and the operators were never identified.

Evidence: Official military incident reports. National Defence declined to comment on specific cases "for operational and security reasons."

Rating: Strong (official records). Likely explanation: small consumer or commercial drones flown by unknown operators. Nothing in the records points to anything exotic; the open question is who flew them and why. Source: CTVNews.ca / CP24

"Jellyfish" object filmed near Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson, Alaska

What was reported: Visitor Sara Jane Osborn says she was photographing a rainbow over Anchorage on June 15, 2026, when she filmed a dark, black-and-silver shape with dangling appendages that appeared to move as it turned. It was visible for about three minutes, in the direction of Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson. The video surfaced online this month and drew new coverage on November 16.

Evidence: Video and still images from a named witness. The footage is low resolution, so distance, altitude and size cannot be measured. No radar data or military statement has been made public.

Rating: Moderate. Likely explanation: a kite or balloon. Commentator Jeremy Corbell said it was most likely a tethered octopus-style show kite, and a former UAP Task Force analyst called it most likely a high-altitude balloon. Osborn disputes the kite explanation. Unexplained so far, but nothing confirmed either way. Source: East Idaho News
