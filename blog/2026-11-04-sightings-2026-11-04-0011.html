Webcam Films Mystery Lights Near Mexico's Popocatépetl Volcano on Day of the Dead

This scan turned up one new report that clears our bar for credibility: lights recorded by a volcano monitoring webcam near Popocatépetl, Mexico, on the night of the Day of the Dead. Several Mexican news outlets covered the footage. Nobody has identified what the lights were, and the people who run the cameras say several ordinary causes fit. Here's what we know.

Lights filmed near Popocatépetl on Day of the Dead night (Nov 2)

What was reported: During the night of Monday, November 2, 2026, a fixed monitoring camera at Tlamacas, Puebla, near the Popocatépetl volcano, recorded several bright points and streaks crossing the sky. According to UnoTV and Eje Central, the lights moved in straight, diagonal paths, some appearing one after another, and some seemed to rise toward the crater. Neither outlet gave an exact time or count.

Evidence: The footage comes from a 24-hour camera feed operated by Webcams de México, which shared the clip on social media. UnoTV, Eje Central and El Heraldo de México all reported on it on November 3. There are no named eyewitnesses, no second camera angle and no radar data. Everything rests on one video.

Likely ordinary explanation: Mexico's disaster prevention agency, CENAPRED, says its night cameras boost brightness and contrast. That can make insects, birds or dust lit up close to the lens look like large glowing objects far away, and it makes size and distance very hard to judge. Satellites catching sunlight and meteors are also possible. Past "UFO" clips from the Popocatépetl cameras have usually been traced to these causes. No official identification has been made for this clip.

Context: The volcano is at Yellow Phase 2 on Mexico's alert scale. CENAPRED logged 60 low-intensity exhalations and 29 minutes of tremor in the 24 hours before the reports, but it has not linked that activity to the lights.

Verifiability: Moderate. The video is real and comes from a known public webcam, and multiple national outlets reported it. But it's a single recording with no independent witnesses, and the camera's own operators point to mundane explanations.
