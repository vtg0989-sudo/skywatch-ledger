Pilots report silver pill-shaped object near 41,000 feet over Ohio

One new report made the cut this scan. It's a pilot account with a photo, newly published even though the flight happened in August. Here's what's on the record and what isn't.

Private jet crew reports a silver, pill-shaped object near 41,000 feet over Ohio

What was reported: Two pilots flying a private jet across the U.S. say they passed a silver, pill-shaped object while cruising at about 41,000 feet over Ohio. They described it as at least 30 feet tall, oriented vertically, with a "highly reflective" surface like polished metal and no seams, fins or other flight-control parts. They put it at roughly 43,000 feet, moving the opposite way. Their distance estimate started at about five miles and was later revised to two or three miles.

Where and when: The flight took place on August 16 at around 8 p.m. ET. One outlet places it near Tiffin, Ohio. The crew was approaching a thunderstorm with tops near 55,000 feet and reported engine icing and moderate turbulence. The account was filed with the National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC) on October 26 and picked up by the Daily Mail and The Express on October 29.

Evidence: One of the pilots, identified only as "J," took an iPhone photo after the object had passed, by which point he estimated it was about seven miles behind. NUFORC investigator Ben Hansen interviewed him. According to the report, Cleveland Center saw nothing on radar, the jet's collision-avoidance system showed no traffic, and NUFORC found no matching planned balloon launches.

Rating: Moderate. The witnesses are pilots who gave a detailed, consistent account and a photo. But they're unnamed, it's a single crew, the photo was taken from several miles away, and nothing independent backs it up: no radar return and no official statement. The report also surfaced more than two months after the flight.

Likely ordinary explanation: A high-altitude balloon is still the leading candidate. Many research and commercial balloons are made of reflective film and wouldn't necessarily show up on ATC radar or TCAS. Not every launch appears in public databases. Hansen says the object's even, untapered shape "presents some challenges to the balloon theory," and the crew questioned whether a balloon could survive near the storm. Neither point rules one out. For now it's unexplained, not unexplainable.
