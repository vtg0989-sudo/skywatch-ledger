Fireball and Boom Over Chile, Starship Lights Over Mexico

Two big sky events lit up Latin America on the night of Friday, November 13. Hundreds of people saw them, they were caught on camera, and both have an explanation from scientists or officials. Here is what was reported and what the evidence shows.

Fireball and loud boom over central-southern Chile

What was reported: At about 21:32 local time on November 13, a bright greenish-blue fireball crossed the sky over Chile. Security cameras and phones filmed it. In the Ñuble and Biobío regions, some witnesses said they heard a loud bang a few seconds after the flash and felt their windows shake. Reports came in from the Santiago Metropolitan Region down to Los Ríos.

Evidence: According to BioBioChile, the GLM lightning sensor on NOAA's GOES-19 weather satellite detected a very bright flash about 15 km north-northwest of Cabrero, near the Ñuble–Biobío border. That spot marks where the object was brightest or broke apart, not necessarily where anything landed.

Likely explanation: Astronomer Rodrigo Herrera Camus of the Universidad de Concepción said it was most likely a rock from space, probably tens of centimeters across, burning up in the atmosphere. Red Geocientífica de Chile called it a bolide (a very bright meteor) and said it found no evidence that fragments reached the ground. Astronomer Francisca Contreras said the boom was probably a sonic shock wave. She ruled out debris from SpaceX's Starship, which launched the same day, because the ship's final pass near Chile was cancelled.

Verifiability: Strong. Many independent witnesses, camera footage and satellite sensor data. (BioBioChile)

Moving lights over Mexico tied to Starship Flight 15

What was reported: On the same evening, people in Querétaro, Puebla, Mexico City and Chiapas shared videos of a bright, moving group of lights with a long trail. Many people online called it a UFO.

Evidence: Videos from several states, plus an official statement. Chiapas' Secretaría de Protección Civil said the light seen over the state's coast came from SpaceX's Starship after its launch from Texas, according to TV Azteca.

Likely explanation: Starship Flight 15 launched from Starbase, Texas, that afternoon at 17:47 Eastern time. TV Azteca reports that the ship reached orbit and deployed Starlink satellites. Rocket exhaust and satellites that are still lit by the sun after dark on the ground often look like glowing, moving formations.

Verifiability: Strong (official source), with a clear ordinary explanation. (TV Azteca)
